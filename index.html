Discovery / Alloy client

An LLM pipeline an accountant can audit

2026 · Alloy client, anonymized · 2–3 discovery calls · Python · Claude · openpyxl · reportlab

Through Alloy, my consultancy, I automated job costing for a Canadian commercial playground distributor. Hours of typing per job became minutes, every number traces back to a document, and together with my other automations the client saves 26+ hours a month.

Through Alloy, my consultancy, I automated job costing for a Canadian commercial playground distributor. The hard part wasn’t the code. It was learning a process that lived in the bookkeeper’s head, from someone who did it by feel and had never had to explain it.

How I ran discovery

  • 2–3 calls with the bookkeeper, understanding the company workflow & their needs out of my automation, and walking through finished job folders
  • Their folders contained invoices, freights, customs, site work, surfacing bills, and quotes. These files were situated as PDFs, scans and Word documents.

What I asked

  • “Walk me through this job. For each bill, what do you calculate, and where?”
  • “Why is the tax on this bill treated differently from that one?”
  • “Which exchange rate do you use, and where does it come from?”
  • “What do you do when a bill hasn’t arrived yet? How would you know one is missing?”

What I learned

Tax and currency looked like they were decided case by case. Walking through real jobs showed otherwise:

  • Sales tax differs by vendor. Some Canadian sales tax (HST) can be claimed back, one carrier charges none, and customs bills mix fees, duty and import tax that are each treated differently.
  • The exchange rate is theirs, not the market’s. It must match what their bank charged, so an automatic rate lookup would have been wrong.
  • Every number must trace to a document in that job’s folder. Nothing is estimated.
  • The folder is never complete. A quote often stands in for an invoice that hasn’t arrived, and a missing bill is only noticed when another document mentions it.

What looked like case-by-case judgment was four firm rules that nobody had written down.

How it shaped the build

Each finding became a design rule. Because every number had to trace to a document, the AI agent only reads the bills and plain Python does every calculation, so the bookkeeper can audit it. The vendor tax rules live in one file, so a policy change is a single edit. Because the folder is never complete, quotes standing in for invoices, bills that other documents name, and any rule I was unsure of are all flagged for review instead of guessed.

Impact

Hours of typing per job became minutes plus a short review list. Together with my other automations for them, the client saves 26+ hours a month. Once running, the automation also flagged a $3,000+ government rebate they had missed more than a year earlier.

“We used to dread doing this because it would take hours out of our day. Now we are so far ahead on our work.”The client

Next: scaling it up

Version one proved the rules with the bookkeeper in the loop. With that trust in place, the next phase is:

  • Pulling bills straight from the shared inbox, so each job is costed as its bills arrive instead of waiting for a folder to be filled.
  • Logging every flag the bookkeeper overrides, so the written rules tighten from real corrections as volume grows.
  • Reusing the pipeline for other distributors. The rules already live in one file, so a new client means writing down their rules, not new code.
Validation / CATTLElytics

Voice tasks for dairy farms, tested with the people who use them

Sep 2025 – Apr 2026 · Capstone with CATTLElytics · Usability study, 5 operators

Through my capstone with CATTLElytics, whose platform supports 1 in 50 dairy cows across North America, I led AI development on a five-engineer team. Pocket AI turns one spoken sentence into a farm task, with 98% recognition under simulated barn noise and a 4.4 out of 5 rating from operators.

Pocket AI turns one spoken sentence into a task for dairy farm workers. I led AI development on our five-person capstone team and wrote our usability report. The challenge was building for people who work with their hands full, in loud barns with weak signal, and who don’t think in terms of software features.

Setting requirements with the client

The CATTLElytics team set scope and priorities with us in regular Teams meetings. That gave us four requirements:

  • Voice first. Operators have their hands full, so a task should take one spoken sentence, not a form.
  • Built for the barn. Recognition had to reach 95% over barn noise.
  • Works without signal. Farms have weak connectivity, so nothing could depend on being online.
  • A focused first release. Multi-language, wearables and advanced NLP moved to stretch goals so the core loop could ship.

What we built

OpenAI Whisper transcribes speech and a language model pipeline pulls the description, deadline, assignee and priority out of a single sentence. A Flask backend handles authentication, task lifecycle and sync with CATTLElytics’ proprietary storage. The app is offline-first: tasks save to local SQLite, sync in the background, and show when they are still waiting to sync. Tasks are assigned to people by name and can repeat on a schedule. Every task goes through a confirmation step before it saves, and anything can be edited by touch.

How I validated it

  • The users: five dairy farm operators, two comfortable with voice assistants and three not.
  • The tasks: operators created real tasks, such as “check Cow 42 for lameness tomorrow morning,” three times each, unaided, with recorded barn noise playing.
  • The debrief: each operator talked through what worked and what they would change.

Results

Recognition held at 98% under simulated barn noise, beating the 95% target. Speech to confirmation took 5.8 seconds on average, and operators rated the app 4.4 out of 5. Saying the description, deadline, assignee and priority in one sentence was what all five liked most. The app was handed off to CATTLElytics for integration into their platform, and the project was highlighted on official McMaster channels.

Next: what I handed off

My usability report closed with a roadmap for the integration:

  • A wake word, for fully hands-free use.
  • Field testing in live barns with more operators.
